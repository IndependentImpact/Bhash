| D-0002 | 2026-03-09 | Adopt canonical instance IRI patterns for Hedera resources                              | Projects consuming the ontology were minting local IRIs for accounts, topics, messages, tokens, and contracts, hurting interoperability and making `owl:sameAs` alignment manual. A deterministic scheme under `https://hashgraphontology.xyz/resource/{network}/{type}/{shard}.{realm}.{num}` was adopted, anchored on native Hedera entity IDs. Topic messages use `…/message/{sequenceNumber}` as the stable sub-key. The patterns are encoded as `hedera:instanceIRIPattern` annotations on each class, enforced with SHACL `sh:Warning` shapes in `ontology/shapes/instance-iris.shacl.ttl`, and illustrated in `ontology/examples/canonical-iris.ttl`. | D-0003 | 2026-10-07 | Model Hiero layers and shards as parts of a network, not subclasses of `hedera:Network` | `hedera:HieroLayer` and `hedera:HieroShard` were declared `rdfs:subClassOf hedera:Network`, so every layer and shard was also a network (and, via `hedera:Network rdfs:subClassOf hedera:Actor`, an actor). A layer is a component of a network and a shard is a partition of one, so the relationship is partonomy, not taxonomy. Both classes now sit directly under `hedera:Concept` and are related to their network only through `hedera:hasLayer` / `hedera:isLayerOf` and `hedera:hasShard` / `hedera:isShardOf`; `hedera:Network` is declared `owl:disjointWith` both, so a layer or shard used where a network is expected (e.g. as subject of `hedera:isGovernedBy`, or object of `hedera:exposedOn`) is now caught by the reasoner and by `tests/verify/core-network-partonomy.rq`. Consequences: `hedera:Network` now means a whole ledger (mainnet, testnet, previewnet), which lets us declare those as named individuals; `hedera:exposedOn` no longer covers shards; the duplicate `hedera:HieroShard` declaration in `hiero.ttl` was removed in favour of the one in `core.ttl` (definitions and source documents merged); the core-consensus example no longer asserts that the Hedera Council governs the shard directly. See [HIP-840](https://hips.hedera.com/hip/hip-840) and the [Hiero overview](https://docs.hedera.com/hiero).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Proposed | Ontology Engineering Team |
| D-0004 | 2026-10-07 | Make `hedera:Network` a subclass of `hedera:Concept` instead of `hedera:Actor`          | `hedera:Network` was declared `rdfs:subClassOf hedera:Actor`, so every network was also a `prov:Agent` and inherited the `hedera:Actor` restriction `hedera:participatesIn some hedera:Process`. A network is the environment in which actors, services and processes operate, not a participant in them: it is related to processes through `hedera:occursOn`, to services through `hedera:exposedOn`, to artefacts through `hedera:registeredIn` and to governance bodies through `hedera:isGovernedBy`. `hedera:Network` now sits directly under `hedera:Concept`. No example or test fixture used a network where an actor is expected, so no instance data had to change.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Proposed | Ontology Engineering Team |
| D-0005 | 2026-10-07 | Declare the public networks and HBAR as named individuals in the core ontology          | Mainnet, testnet, previewnet and HBAR are fixed, universal instances that the ontology itself needs to refer to, but nothing declared them, and HBAR has no entity ID, so it cannot be minted under the D-0002 `https://hashgraphontology.xyz/resource/{network}/{type}/{shard}.{realm}.{num}` scheme. `core.ttl` now declares `hedera:Mainnet`, `hedera:Testnet` and `hedera:Previewnet` as `owl:NamedIndividual`s of `hedera:Network` (pairwise distinct via `owl:AllDifferent`), each with a `skos:notation` equal to its `{network}` IRI segment, a ledger ID (`hedera:hasLedgerId`, [HIP-198](https://hips.hedera.com/hip/hip-198)), an EVM chain ID (`hedera:hasEvmChainId`; 295, 296, 297) and `hedera:hasNativeCurrency hedera:HBAR`. HBAR is an individual of the new class `hedera:NativeCurrency`, under a new `hedera:DigitalAsset` that `hedera:Token` now also specialises; `hedera:Token` is `owl:disjointWith hedera:NativeCurrency`, so typing HBAR as a token is caught. One HBAR individual serves all networks; testnet HBAR having no monetary value is a property of the network, not a different currency. The individuals live in the `core/` namespace rather than under `/resource/`, because they are vocabulary constants versioned with the ontology and resolvable through `/core` content negotiation, while `/resource/` is reserved for producer-minted instances. Testnet resets mean a `/resource/testnet/` IRI can denote different entities before and after a reset; this is recorded as a `skos:scopeNote` on `hedera:Testnet` but not otherwise modelled yet. Checked by `tests/verify/core-network-individuals.rq`. | Proposed | Ontology Engineering Team |

## How to propose updates

1. **Draft entry** – add a new row with a unique ID (`D-XXXX`), provisional status (`Proposed`), and reference links.
2. **Review** – discuss in issues or pull requests; capture alternatives considered.
3. **Finalise** – once approved, update the status to `Accepted` or `Rejected` and reference the decision artefact.

Maintain chronological order and ensure each decision points back to the relevant sources in `docs/references/bibliography.md`.
