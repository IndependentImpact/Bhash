| D-0003 | 2026-10-07 | Model Hiero layers and shards as parts of a network, not subclasses of `hedera:Network`. `hedera:HieroLayer` and `hedera:HieroShard` were declared `rdfs:subClassOf hedera:Network`, so every layer and shard was also a network (and, via `hedera:Network rdfs:subClassOf hedera:Actor`, an actor). A layer is a component of a network and a shard is a partition of one, so the relationship is partonomy, not taxonomy. Both classes now sit directly under `hedera:Concept` and are related to their network only through `hedera:hasLayer` / `hedera:isLayerOf` and `hedera:hasShard` / `hedera:isShardOf`; `hedera:Network` is declared `owl:disjointWith` both, so a layer or shard used where a network is expected (e.g. as subject of `hedera:isGovernedBy`, or object of `hedera:exposedOn`) is now caught by the reasoner and by `tests/verify/core-network-partonomy.rq`. Consequences: `hedera:Network` now means a whole ledger (mainnet, testnet, previewnet), which lets us declare those as named individuals; `hedera:exposedOn` no longer covers shards; the duplicate `hedera:HieroShard` declaration in `hiero.ttl` was removed in favour of the one in `core.ttl` (definitions and source documents merged); the core-consensus example no longer asserts that the Hedera Council governs the shard directly. See [HIP-840](https://hips.hedera.com/hip/hip-840) and the [Hiero overview](https://docs.hedera.com/hiero). | Proposed                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Ontology Engineering Team |

## How to propose updates

1. **Draft entry** – add a new row with a unique ID (`D-XXXX`), provisional status (`Proposed`), and reference links.
2. **Review** – discuss in issues or pull requests; capture alternatives considered.
3. **Finalise** – once approved, update the status to `Accepted` or `Rejected` and reference the decision artefact.

Maintain chronological order and ensure each decision points back to the relevant sources in `docs/references/bibliography.md`.
